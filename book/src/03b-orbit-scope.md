# Orbit Scope

The orbit scope is the first major shift where the player leaves the planet without leaving industry behind.

This scope should not feel like a separate genre. It is still an industrial game, but now the industrial body exists in orbit. The station becomes both a logistics mediator and a production site in its own right.

## Core fantasy

The fantasy of the orbit scope is:

- lift industry off the planet
- make orbit productive
- build a machine that survives through external structure and internal order

## Why the orbit scope needs two layers

The orbit has an outside and an inside for design reasons, not only visual reasons.

- the orbit layer (O1) is about the orbital environment — the station as seen from outside, the planet below (if any), docking, shuttle traffic, and the relationship between orbital and surface industry
- the station layer (O2) is about the station internals — manufacturing, processing, storage, and controlled industrial density within the station hull

If both are collapsed into one layer, the station risks becoming either an abstract management menu or just another factory map. The two-layer model prevents that flattening.

## Orbit composition

Every orbit contains exactly one space station. Some orbits also have a planet nearby.

- **Station only**: the orbit is a pure outpost — an asteroid mining hub, a route junction, or a strategic relay. No planet to descend to.
- **Station + planet**: the orbit includes both a station and a planet below. The player can enter the station (→ O2) or descend to the planet surface (→ P2).

Not every orbit has a planet. This is important — it means some stations exist purely as orbital infrastructure, independent of any planetary economy.

## Planet access through orbit

Before star gates (epochs 1–6), a planet is only reachable from its parent orbit. The player must have a station in orbit to look into, land on, or supply a planet.

This creates a deliberate dependency chain:

1. reach the orbit (from S1 star layer)
2. build or capture a station there
3. only then can the planet below be explored

After star gates (epoch 7+), this restriction lifts. Star gates allow direct travel from S2 to any planet, bypassing the station requirement.

## Station role in the campaign

The station is where the player learns that logistics can no longer be solved only through local adjacency.

The player now has to care about:

- launch cadence
- orbital storage
- docking pressure
- construction sequence
- what is worth producing in orbit
- how surface and orbit specialize relative to each other

## Main station tensions

The orbit scope is built around a few powerful tensions:

- external expansion versus internal efficiency
- exposed infrastructure versus protected manufacturing
- planetary dependency versus orbital self-sufficiency
- throughput ambition versus catastrophic fragility

The station should always feel like a valuable but vulnerable machine.
